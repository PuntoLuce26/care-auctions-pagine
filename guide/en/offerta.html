How to bid at an Italian court auction: deposit, minimum bid and form

In a judicial auction you do not buy by outbidding an auctioneer in a room: you file a written bid before the auction. Prepared properly, half the work is already done.

1 · Starting price and minimum bid

Every lot carries two figures: the starting price and the minimum bid, which is 75% of it. Below the minimum bid the envelope is not even opened: it is the legal floor, not a discount. Mixing the two up is the most common mistake — market statistics are always read on the starting price.

Source: PVP — lot page with starting price and minimum bid

2 · The deposit

The deposit is paid before the bid, exactly as the sale notice prescribes: usually 10% of the starting price, but the notice governs. Bank draft, transfer or deposit: proof of payment must be inside the envelope, or the bid is inadmissible.

Source: Sale notice published by the court (on the PVP portal)

3 · The form and the envelope

The bid is filed on a form stating identity, the price offered, the lot, the balance payment method and the attachments (deposit, ID, power of attorney if any). Online auctions are filed on the portal named in the notice; otherwise at the court registry or before the delegated notary, before the deadline.

Source: Italian Ministry of Justice — how to take part in an auction

4 · Who cannot bid

The debtor, bankrupt parties and anyone who worked on the procedure (valuer, custodian, delegate) cannot bid. You may bid through a lawyer with a special power of attorney.

Source: Italian Code of Civil Procedure, arts. 571-579

5 · After filing

A bid cannot be withdrawn. If nobody bids, the judge orders a new attempt at a reduced price (usually up to 25% less). If several bids arrive, the auction opens among the bidders as the judge directs.

Source: PVP — outcome and next attempt of the procedure

The official sources

Everything here can be checked on public sources: the Italian Ministry of Justice Public Sales Portal publishes notices, valuation reports and outcomes of every procedure; giustizia.it explains sales and auctions; the law is on Normattiva.
