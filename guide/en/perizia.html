Reading the valuation report: what it contains and what to check before bidding

The valuation report decides whether the deal is real. Whoever skips it buys a house they have not seen, with defects they did not ask for.

1 · Who writes it and what it is worth

It is the report of the court-appointed expert (CTU): it describes the property, its condition, its history and the appraised value. It is filed with the procedure documents and read together with the sale notice.

Source: PVP — attachments of the procedure (report as PDF)

2 · What it contains

Land-registry identification, size (sqm, rooms), condition, systems, building and registry irregularities, occupation (free or occupied, by whom and under what title), chain of title, encumbrances, condominium arrears and appraised value.

Source: Procedure documents published on the PVP

3 · Irregularities

Building abuses and registry mismatches are on the buyer: the report lists them and, where fixable, gives the cost of regularisation. An unfixable irregularity makes the property hard to resell and to mortgage.

Source: Valuation report — irregularity section

4 · Occupation

An occupied property costs time and money to free. The report states whether it is free or occupied and how delivery can be obtained: it is one of the heaviest items in the price you should bid.

Source: Valuation report — occupation; sale notice

5 · From appraised value to maximum bid

The appraised value is not the right price: subtract works, regularisations, condominium arrears, purchase taxes, fees and waiting time. What remains is your maximum bid: above it the deal stops being a deal, even if you "win" the auction.

Source: Turnkey Price — the real cost of an auction (CAReAuctions)

The official sources

Everything here can be checked on public sources: the Italian Ministry of Justice Public Sales Portal publishes notices, valuation reports and outcomes of every procedure; giustizia.it explains sales and auctions; the law is on Normattiva.
