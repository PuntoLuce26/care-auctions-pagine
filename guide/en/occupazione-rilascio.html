Occupied property: what it means, what it costs and how it is freed

An occupied property can cost months and thousands of euros. It is not a detail of the report: it is the item that changes the price worth bidding.

1 · Vacant or occupied: where to read it

Occupation is stated in the sale notice and in the valuation report: vacant, occupied by the debtor, occupied by third parties under a contract (lease, loan for use). The occupier's title changes everything.

Source: Valuation report and sale notice — occupation status

2 · What the decree provides

The transfer decree orders delivery. If the property is occupied without title, possession is taken with the judicial officer; if there is an enforceable lease, timing stretches to its expiry.

Source: Transfer decree — delivery provisions

3 · Enforceable lease

A lease registered before the seizure may be enforceable against the buyer: you inherit the contract and the rent until expiry. This must be checked in the report before bidding, not after.

Source: Valuation report — contracts and chain of title

4 · The real cost of freeing it

Legal fees, judicial officer, waiting time, possible custody and repairs: certain costs with uncertain timing. They belong in the turnkey calculation as a separate, prudently estimated item.

Source: Turnkey Price — costs and timing (CAReAuctions)

5 · How to bid on an occupied property

You bid less: the price drops by the cost and time of freeing it, not by the value of the property. Whoever skips this calculation pays a vacant-property price and discovers the cost afterwards.

Source: CAReAuctions method — real discount vs OMI values, with coverage
