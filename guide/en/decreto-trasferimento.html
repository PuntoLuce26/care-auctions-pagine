The transfer decree: when ownership actually changes hands

The transfer decree is what makes you the owner: until then you hold a right, not a house. Knowing how it works avoids surprises on timing and delivery.

1 · When it arrives

Once the balance is paid, the judge issues the transfer decree: it transfers ownership to the buyer and orders the release of encumbrances. Timing depends on the court and its workload.

Source: PVP — status of the procedure after payment

2 · What it contains

Identification of the property, the buyer, the price, the chain of title, the occupation status and the orders cancelling prior charges (mortgages, seizures). Read it line by line: it is the title you will take to the notary and the bank.

Source: Transfer decree (documents of the procedure)

3 · Registration

The decree is registered in the land registers: from then ownership is enforceable against third parties. Registration is handled by the delegate or the buyer as the order provides; without it the position stays fragile.

Source: Land registry — registration of the decree

4 · Delivery of the property

The decree also orders delivery: a vacant property is handed over with the keys; an occupied one follows the order's rules (taking possession, with the judicial officer where needed).

Source: Decree — delivery provisions

5 · The costs of the transfer

Registration and mortgage taxes, delegate fees, registration and cancellation costs are paid here. Whoever built the full picture before bidding already put them in the price.

Source: Italian Revenue Agency — taxes and formalities

The official sources

Everything here can be checked on public sources: the Italian Ministry of Justice Public Sales Portal publishes notices, valuation reports and outcomes of every procedure; giustizia.it explains sales and auctions; the law is on Normattiva.
